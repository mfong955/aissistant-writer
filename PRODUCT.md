# Product

## Register

both

Both surfaces carry equal weight. The landing page (`/`) drives conversion with brand-register intent — design IS the first impression. The writing app (`/project`, `/projects`) is product-register — design SERVES the work. They share one visual identity; neither subordinates to the other.

## Platform

web

## Users

**Primary**: Serious fiction writers — novelists, short story writers, worldbuilders, people tracking dozens of characters, timelines, and settings across a long project. They've hit the context wall with generic AI tools: pasting backstory on every session, repeating themselves, losing continuity. They want a tool that holds the whole story so they don't have to.

**Accessible to all**: The feature set and interface must not require a technical background or writing expertise. First-novel writers and hobby creators are valid users; the product earns their trust through clarity, not simplicity theater.

**Secondary surface audience**: The landing page speaks to anyone who has struggled with AI tools losing project context — the frustration drives the conversion.

## Product Purpose

Aissistant Writer is an AI writing studio for long-form projects. It gives writers a persistent, project-aware AI partner that has read everything in their world — characters, chapters, settings, notes — so conversations stay grounded and never need re-explanation. The core value is the elimination of the context problem: you build the project once, the AI knows it forever.

Success looks like a writer finishing a long project they would have abandoned — because the AI kept pace.

## Positioning

An AI partner that knows your story, helps with ideas, and organizes everything — so you can focus on the writing, not on managing context.

## Brand Personality

**Intelligent · Alive · Collaborative.** The product should feel like a smart co-writer in the room — aware, responsive, and genuinely engaged with your story. Not a productivity tool. Not a chatbot. A partner.

Voice is confident but not precious. It knows writing is serious work and treats it that way. No cheerleader energy, no hollow enthusiasm. The tone earns trust through precision and capability, not warmth marketing.

"Alive" is achieved through intelligence (responses that show it actually read your project) and responsiveness (the interface reacts to what you're doing), not through animation noise or decorative motion.

## Anti-references

**Primary**: Scrivener / legacy writing software. Dense toolbar-heavy UIs, skeuomorphic cork boards, windows inside windows. Everything competes for attention. The writer never feels like they have a clean surface to work on.

**Secondary**: Generic AI SaaS 2024–2026. Cream or sand backgrounds, purple gradient accents, hero metric cards, "01 / 02 / 03" numbered sections, gradient text headings. Looks like 50 other tools.

**Tertiary**: Sudowrite's dreamy aesthetic — soft gradients, whimsical typography. Beautiful but too precious for serious long-form work. Writers who are 80,000 words into a novel need focus, not vibes.

## Design Principles

**Intelligent presence, light touch.** The AI is always nearby — chat panel, inline toolbar, context awareness — but the interface recedes to make the writing surface feel open and undistracted. The product's intelligence shows through results, not UI chrome.

**Surface the story, not the tool.** Every UI element competes with the writing. Default to less. Only what earns its place survives. The explorer, chat, and toolbar exist to serve the manuscript, never to impress.

**Alive through capability.** "Alive" means the product responds to what you're doing — inline actions appear on selection, the AI knows which chapter you're in, context updates as you write. Motion and micro-interactions are purposeful, not decorative.

**Organized like a collaborator.** The entity system (characters, chapters, settings) mirrors how a thoughtful writing partner would organize a project. Structure feels earned and familiar, not imposed.

**Approachable depth.** Powerful enough for a novelist managing 30 characters across 5 acts. Clear enough for someone writing their first story. Progressive disclosure wins over feature sprawl.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA compliance. Screen reader support (VoiceOver, NVDA). Semantic HTML throughout. Keyboard navigation for all interactive surfaces. The writing environment especially must be fully navigable without a mouse.
